01

Meet the qubit

A qubit is the quantum version of a bit. It can be prepared in a state that gives a probability of measuring 0 or 1.

Click the button to perform a measurement. The state is not secretly both classical values—it is a quantum state whose measurement has probabilistic outcomes.

A measurement is an interaction. It changes the qubit from a spread-out quantum state into one definite result.

Superposition

|ψ⟩ = 0.71|0⟩ + 0.71|1⟩

Superposition means a qubit can have amplitudes for both |0⟩ and |1⟩ before measurement.

Drag the state around the Bloch-sphere sketch. The angle changes the balance of probabilities: at the top it is definitely 0; at the bottom, definitely 1; halfway, it is an equal superposition.

Amplitudes can be positive, negative, and even complex. Their squared magnitudes become measurement probabilities.
03

Entanglement

Entangled qubits share one joint quantum state. Measuring one gives information about the other, even when separated.

This does not let us send a message faster than light. The outcomes are correlated, but each individual result is still random.

Interference

Quantum algorithms steer amplitudes so wrong answers cancel and useful answers reinforce.

Think of waves: peaks meeting peaks grow; peaks meeting troughs disappear. Quantum gates manipulate the wave-like amplitudes of computational paths.

Aligned paths add up, making the target outcome more likely.
